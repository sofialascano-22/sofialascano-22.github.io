Research · Asatekin Lab, Tufts · Summer Scholars 2025 – present

Heavy-metal removal with selective, fouling-resistant polymer membranes

Membranes can separate ions using far less energy than distillation, which matters because conventional chemical separations use about 15% of global energy. I make zwitterionic copolymer membranes and test how crosslinking changes what they reject.

What I did

  • Rod-cast thin-film composite membranes from two zwitterionic copolymers (PAMT and PAS) onto a porous support.
  • Crosslinked the selective layer with a thiol and thermal initiator, and compared a heat gun against a heat lamp.
  • Ran dead-end filtration at 50 psi with CuCl₂, NiCl₂, NaCl and MgCl₂, and measured rejection by ICP and conductivity.
  • Measured film swelling and salt partitioning to explain the results.

Results

Coursework · ChBE 39 Applied Numerical Methods, Tufts · December 2025

Modeling CO₂ capture for a campus boiler with a calcium hydroxide slurry

The Science and Technology Center at Tufts heats with a natural-gas boiler that emits about 56 kg of CO₂ an hour. We modeled a slurry scrubber that could capture it without replacing the boiler.

What we did

  • Estimated the boiler's CO₂ output (1,273 mol/h) from the building's fiscal-year 2024 energy data.
  • Wrote and solved the coupled gas- and droplet-phase transport equations in Python.
  • Swept temperature, gas void fraction and droplet radius against a 90% capture target.
  • Sized the reactor and the sorbent needed for an 8-hour shift.

Results

1.06 m³reactor volume at a 4.9 s gas contact time
679 kgCa(OH)₂ for 90% capture over 8 h

Coursework · BME 175 Tissue Engineering, Tufts · December 2025

A modular, 3D-printed perfusion bioreactor that models the GI tract

Most lab models of the gut cover one or two segments. We designed a bioreactor whose chambers connect in series, each holding a silk scaffold, so cells can be grown under flow in conditions closer to the body.

What we did

  • Designed the wells, lids and base in SolidWorks over several iterations, aiming for a leak-free, modular build with little wasted resin or media.
  • Compared three printing resins and chose BioMed Clear for its biocompatibility and sterilizability.
  • Prepared silk fibroin solution and porous sponge scaffolds (about 6% fibroin).
  • Sealed each chamber with gaskets and a four-bolt clamp, then ran perfusion tests.
